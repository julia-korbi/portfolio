01 · Data Integrity & Attribution

Making the funnel tell the truth

Attribution logic, database health, and lifecycle rules that Sales and Marketing can both trust without a manual audit.

ATTRIBUTION · SALESFORCE

Opportunity Attribution Engine

Sales hand-picked deal source on every opportunity. It needed manual correction roughly 80% of the time — call it a rolling audit, not a report.

Before3 hrs/wk manual cleanup→30-min health check
▸ View full breakdown
MechanismReads Campaign Member logs against the opportunity's Primary Contact Role: a Source-type touch inside a 90-day window before opportunity creation auto-populates Opportunity Source + Source Campaign; an Influence-type or out-of-window touch attributes to influence only; anything ambiguous falls into a flagged manual-review queue instead of silently guessing.
GuardrailsValidation rules require Deal Type and a Primary Contact Role at opportunity creation, so the flow never has to fall back on missing data.
OutcomeFunnel reporting became representative and realistic without sales guesswork — and the model shipped live, not handed off at spec stage.
DATA GOVERNANCE · HUBSPOT

Database Cleanup & Health Dashboard

450 HubSpot properties, most undocumented or unused, and no way to check database health without diving into raw records.

450 props→ 140 essential→1 health dashboard
▸ View full breakdown
ActionAudited every property, retired what wasn't earning its place, and rebuilt the automation logic that depended on the fields being removed — so cleanup didn't break anything downstream.
Companion systemAutomated Engagement Status → Marketing/Non-marketing contact logic on priority-ordered rules (suppression checks first, then engagement signals), keeping the HubSpot marketing-contact limit managed without manual review.
OutcomeA 450 → 140 property reduction and a single dashboard that answers "is the database healthy?" without opening a single record.
LIFECYCLE · HUBSPOT

Lifecycle Stage Redesign

Duplicate stage values, no documented rules for using them, and leads stuck permanently in "Cadence" or "Nurture" with nobody owning the next step.

BeforeDuplicate, undocumented stages→One clear map
▸ View full breakdown
ActionAudited and removed unused/duplicate values, then separated the model into two layers: Lifecycle Stage as a forward-only record of "how far this contact has ever progressed," and Lead/Sales Status as the cyclical "what's happening right now" — never conflating the two.
MechanismExplicit next-step logic at every status, plus a defined re-engagement path so "stuck in nurture" stopped being a permanent state.
OutcomeSales and Marketing now share one transparent answer to "where is this lead, and what happens next" — no more silent dead ends.

SLA ALERTING

Tiered SLA Alerting

The highest-intent actions — 1:1 demo requests, in-browser demos — had no enforced response discipline, and leadership had no visibility into whether they were actually being worked (Ops included).

Cadence10 min → 12 hr → 24 hr→Leadership CC
▸ View full breakdown
ActionBuilt escalating first-response alerts on the highest-intent triggers, reminding at 10 minutes, 12 hours, and 24 hours, and CC'ing leadership once a reminder was missed.
Design choiceAlerts route to Sales and Ops, not just the rep, and escalate to leadership on a miss — so the whole chain can see how leads are actually being worked instead of trusting a lagging report.
OutcomeTurned "someone should probably follow up fast" into an enforced, auditable SLA on the actions that matter most. It also reshaped how MQL volume was counted: an MQL was credited once the highest-intent action — a completed demo — actually closed, giving Sales a cleaner signal than looser criteria had, and feeding directly into the SAL→SQO conversion gains covered above.
NURTURE ARCHITECTURE · HUBSPOT

Multi-Track Nurture Pacing Engine

Persona-specific automated nurture and the always-on scheduled send calendar could both enroll the same contact independently — with no shared limit on how often that person actually got emailed.

BeforeTwo tracks, no shared cap→2–3 touches/wk, shared
▸ View full breakdown
ActionBuilt separate nurture tracks for different personas, designed to run in parallel with the regular scheduled send calendar rather than compete with it for the same inbox.
MechanismEvery send — automated or scheduled — checks the contact's last marketing email send date before firing, holding the message until it clears a shared frequency ceiling. Both tracks can process the same contact simultaneously, capped at 2–3 total touches per week regardless of which one is sending.
VisibilityBuilt a dashboard showing every enrolled contact's current position across active tracks and remaining send capacity before the next touch, so pacing was a visible, managed constraint rather than a guess.
OutcomePersona-relevant nurture and the always-on send calendar ran at the same time without fatiguing or overloading the database.

03 · AI & Automation

Where AI replaced manual work — with a defined mechanism

Each case names what problem existed, why AI was the right tool, and what it actually changed — not just that AI was "leveraged."

AI SDR · QUALIFIED

AI SDR, built in Qualified

Every inbound lead needed personalized first-touch follow-up before a human SDR could jump in — and dropped demo-request forms were eating SDR time in manual chasing.

Impact70% less manual follow-up→AI-first, human handoff
▸ View full breakdown
MechanismThe agent pulls context automatically from CRM properties — company, tech stack, assigned persona, webinar/event attendance and the questions asked there, known account pain points — and uses it to nurture the lead through highly personalized outreach with zero SDR involvement until first response.
HandoffOnce the lead responds, it's handed to a live SDR for the actual conversation and meeting — the AI owns the nurture, not the close.
OutcomeRoughly 70% less manual time spent on marketing follow-up and chasing dropped demo requests.

Templates, Built With a Claude Skill

BeforeOne-person coding queue→Claude Skill, self-serve
▸ View full breakdown
Why AIThe templates, brand rules, and tracking requirements were already well-defined and repeatable — a structured task suited to a packaged AI skill rather than a one-off manual build every time.

A marketer describes what they need in plain language; the skill generates a ready-to-ship, on-brand HTML build — no developer, no ticket, no waiting on one person's calendar.
GuardrailsThe skill enforces brand styling, tracking parameters, and responsive layout every time, so self-serve speed doesn't come at the cost of consistency or broken tracking.
OutcomeEvery channel team gained real independence in content production, while every output still matched brand and tracking standards.
AI OPS · CLAUDE

Bulk Tracking Rollout via Claude

Thousands of live HubSpot registration forms needed new tracking fields added — one at a time, by hand, with zero margin for inconsistency.

BeforeDays of manual form edits→1 prompt · ~30 min
▸ View full breakdown
